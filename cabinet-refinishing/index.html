Refinishing and refacing solve different problems. Refacing gives you new doors in a new style. Refinishing keeps the doors you have and changes their color. If your cabinets are solid-wood raised panel or a clean shaker profile you genuinely like, and the only complaint is that the honey-oak or cherry stain feels dated, refinishing gets you most of the visual change for meaningfully less money.

The work is almost entirely in the preparation. Kitchen doors carry years of cooking grease, and finish will not bond through it. We degrease thoroughly, sand to a mechanical profile, and use a bonding primer rated for cabinetry - not wall paint. Finish coats are sprayed rather than brushed so the surface is even and free of brush marks. Face frames and exposed panels are finished in place with containment, and doors are labeled so every one returns to the opening it came from and hangs correctly.

Good candidates

Solid-wood doors, oak, maple, cherry, and birch, in sound condition with tight joints. Thermofoil doors are not candidates - the vinyl skin delaminates and will not hold a coating reliably. Doors with split rails, delaminated veneer, or water swelling at the bottom edge should be replaced instead, which puts you back into refacing for those pieces.

Grain and color realities

Two honest notes. First, oak has open grain, and a painted finish will still show that texture. Many people like it; some expect glass-smooth and are disappointed. We show you a finished oak sample up front so you decide with real information. Second, going dramatically lighter over a dark stain takes more coats and more time than going darker, and we price accordingly.

Cost and timeline

Most Dane County refinishing projects run $2,500 to $6,000 depending on door count and how big a color change you are making. Plan on roughly one week, part of which your doors are off while they cure. Cabinet interiors stay usable, and we can stage the work so the kitchen is never fully unavailable.
