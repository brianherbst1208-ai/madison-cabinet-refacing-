We work across all of Madison, and the kitchen we walk into varies enormously by neighborhood. On the near west side - Nakoma, Westmorland, Sunset Village, Monroe Street - we see a lot of 1920s and 1930s bungalows where the kitchen has already been remodeled once or twice. Those kitchens often have solid, site-built cabinetry with real face frames that is genuinely worth keeping, plus a layout that has been reworked to something functional. Refacing those is a strong outcome: new shaker doors and drawer fronts over well-built boxes, with the original character of the room intact.

Head out Midvale, Odana, and into Hill Farms and Midvale Heights and the story changes to postwar ranches. Those kitchens frequently still carry 1960s birch or 1990s honey oak, and the boxes are usually plywood and dead square. This is refacing's sweet spot. A flat slab or clean shaker door in a warm white transforms a 1960s ranch kitchen without touching a layout that, for a galley of that era, generally works. On the east side around Schenk-Atwood and Eken Park, we see a similar mix of small bungalow kitchens where every inch matters, and modifications - pull-outs, a drawer bank, cabinetry extended to the ceiling - often matter as much as the refacing itself.

Downtown and near-campus condos are their own category. Access is the project constraint, not the cabinetry: freight elevators, reserved loading windows, and building rules about work hours. We plan around that, and the compact scale of a condo kitchen means most of these projects install in two or three days.

What Madison kitchens tend to need

Two patterns repeat. First, older Madison homes near the lakes see real seasonal humidity swings, and kitchens go from very dry January furnace air to humid August. We spec door constructions and finishes that tolerate that movement rather than opening at the joints after a heating season. Second, storage in these homes is almost always the actual complaint underneath the aesthetic one. A Madison kitchen with eighteen linear feet of cabinetry and no pull-outs feels much smaller than it is, so we usually quote refacing alongside a couple of interior modifications.

How we work in Madison

Madison is our home city, so the free in-home measure is easy to schedule, and we bring physical door and finish samples to your kitchen. That matters more here than people expect: the same white door reads completely differently in a north-facing Nakoma kitchen than in a west-facing condo with afternoon lake glare. You will get a written quote after we measure, and we will tell you if your boxes are not worth refacing.
