Cabinet Refacing

Cabinet refacing keeps your existing cabinet boxes and replaces everything you see: doors, drawer fronts, face frames, and exposed end panels. It is the fastest way to get a new-kitchen look without a demolition project.

In a typical Madison kitchen, the cabinet boxes are the most expensive component and the part in the best shape. Plywood or solid-frame boxes installed in the 1970s through the 2000s are usually square, solidly attached, and good for decades more. What makes the kitchen feel old is the door profile, the orange-toned finish, and the hardware - all of which refacing replaces.

Here is what actually happens. We take off every door and drawer front and haul them away. Face frames and any exposed end panels are cleaned, prepped, and covered in new veneer or laminate that matches your chosen door finish, so the boxes visually disappear into the new look. New doors and drawer fronts - built to the openings we measured, not trimmed on site - go on with new soft-close hinges. Hardware is drilled from a template so every handle lines up. When we are done, the only original parts still visible are ones that now match.

Door styles and finishes

Shaker remains the most requested style in Dane County by a wide margin, usually in a warm white, a soft greige, or a deep navy on a lower run. Flat slab doors suit downtown condos and mid-century west-side ranches. Raised panel still fits traditional homes in Maple Bluff and Nakoma. You can also mix - painted uppers with a wood-grain lower run reads current and hides the wear that lower cabinets always take first.

How long it takes

Two to four weeks from signed quote to install while your doors are built, then three to five days on site for most kitchens. Your sink, dishwasher, and stove stay connected and usable the entire time. There is no countertop removal, no plumbing disconnect, and no drywall repair - which is exactly why the timeline and the invoice both come in well under a full replacement.

When refacing is not the answer

We will say so at the measure. Particleboard boxes swelling at the sink base, frames that have racked out of square, water damage under the dishwasher, or a layout you actively dislike are all reasons to consider replacement instead. Refacing a bad layout just gives you a nicer-looking bad layout, and we would rather lose the job than sell you that.
