Kitchen Islands

The island is the piece of a kitchen people bump into, sit at, and complain about most. We modify existing islands - extending an overhang, adding seating, changing the finish, adding storage or outlets - and build new ones where the footprint allows.

Islands in Dane County kitchens fall into a few recognizable patterns. Builder islands from 1990s and 2000s subdivisions in Sun Prairie and Verona are usually a plain box with a one-inch overhang - no place to actually sit. Islands in older Madison homes were often added later and are undersized or sitting in the wrong place. Newer islands are frequently fine structurally and just wear the same dated finish as the rest of the kitchen.

Seating is the most common request

Making an island seatable means an overhang of roughly twelve inches minimum, and fifteen is much more comfortable. Extending a stone countertop that far requires real support - corbels, brackets, or a concealed steel plate depending on the material and how much of a clean underside you want. We work directly with countertop fabricators on the extension, because a stone overhang engineered incorrectly cracks along its unsupported edge, and that is not a repair.

Clearances decide what is possible

Before any island change, we measure walkways. Thirty-six inches of clear aisle is the workable minimum; forty-two is what you want on the working side with a dishwasher or oven door swinging into it. The honest answer in some kitchens is that the island is already as large as the room allows, and the useful change is storage and finish rather than size. We would rather tell you that than sell you an island you have to squeeze past every day.

Storage, style, and power

Island interiors are usually underbuilt - one or two big shelved compartments. Converting that into drawers, pull-outs, and a dedicated trash pull-out changes daily use substantially. On the visible side, islands are the natural place to break from the perimeter cabinets: a deep green or navy island against warm white perimeter cabinetry is the most-requested combination we install. Decorative panels, beadboard, or furniture legs make the island read as a piece of furniture rather than a cabinet run. If you need outlets or under-counter lighting added, we coordinate that with a licensed electrician as part of the project.
