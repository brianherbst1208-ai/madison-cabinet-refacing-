What We Do

Cabinet Services We Offer

Refacing is the core of what we do, but not every kitchen needs the same fix. Some cabinets need new doors, some just need a fresh finish, and some need the layout changed. Here is how we approach each one.

Cabinet Refacing

Cabinet refacing keeps your existing cabinet boxes and replaces everything you see: doors, drawer fronts, face frames, and exposed end panels. It is the fastest way to get a new-kitchen look without a demolition project.

Learn more

Cabinet Refinishing

Refinishing keeps your existing doors and drawer fronts and changes their finish. If you like your door style but not its color, this is the least invasive and least expensive option on the table.

Learn more

Doors & Drawer Fronts

Sometimes only the moving parts have failed. Replacing doors, drawer fronts, and worn drawer boxes gets you a current look and drawers that actually work, without refacing the whole kitchen.

Learn more

Cabinet Modifications

Refacing changes how cabinets look. Modifications change how they work - converting dead shelf space into pull-outs, building a drawer bank, removing a cabinet to open a sightline, or fitting cabinetry around a new appliance.

Learn more

Kitchen Islands

The island is the piece of a kitchen people bump into, sit at, and complain about most. We modify existing islands - extending an overhang, adding seating, changing the finish, adding storage or outlets - and build new ones where the footprint allows.

Why Homeowners Choose Us

Why Madison Homeowners Choose Refacing

Most kitchens we look at do not need to be gutted. The boxes are sound, the layout is workable, and what actually reads as dated is the door style, the finish, and the hardware. Refacing replaces exactly that - which is why it comes in well under a full remodel and why you are back to normal in under a week.

  • Roughly half the cost of new cabinets — Cabinet boxes are the expensive part of a kitchen. If yours are solid - and in most Dane County homes built after 1970 they are - refacing puts the money into what you actually see and touch.
  • Days of disruption instead of weeks — A full cabinet replacement means demolition, countertop removal, plumbing and electrical, drywall repair, and often new flooring. Refacing skips nearly all of it. Your sink and stove stay connected.
  • Your layout, kept or improved — If the layout works, we leave it alone. If it does not, we modify it - a deeper drawer bank, a wider island overhang, a pantry pull-out, or a cabinet removed to open a sightline.
  • Honest answers, including no — Particleboard boxes that are swelling at the sink base, frames that are racked, or a layout you genuinely dislike are not refacing candidates. We will tell you that at the free measure rather than after deposit.
  • Built for Wisconsin humidity swings — Madison kitchens go from bone-dry January furnace air to humid August. We use finishes and door constructions that tolerate that movement instead of cracking at the joints after one heating season.
  • One crew, start to finish — The person who measures your kitchen is involved through install. No handing your project to a subcontractor who has never seen the space or heard what you asked for.

Simple Process

From Quote to Done in Four Steps

1. Free in-home measure

We look at your actual cabinet boxes, check whether the frames and hinges are sound, measure every opening, and tell you honestly whether refacing, refinishing, or replacement is the right call for your kitchen.

2. Pick doors, finish, and hardware

Shaker, slab, raised panel, or glass insert - in painted finishes, wood-grain laminate, or real wood veneer. We bring samples to your kitchen so you see them in your own light, next to your own counters and floors.

3. Precision order and build

Your doors and drawer fronts are built to your measured openings. Nothing is cut to fit on your driveway. Lead time is typically two to four weeks, and we schedule the install only once the parts are in hand.

4. Install in days, not months

Old doors come off, face frames and end panels get new matching veneer or laminate, new doors and drawer fronts go on, and hardware is drilled to a template. Most kitchens take three to five days.

5. Walkthrough and adjustment

We adjust every hinge so doors sit flush and close evenly, clean up fully, and walk the kitchen with you. If a door is a hair off six weeks later, we come back and tune it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Cabinet refacing is quoted per project, because the price follows door count, door style, and finish - not square footage of your kitchen. As a working range for Dane County: most refacing projects run about $150 to $400 per linear foot of cabinetry, which puts a typical 25-to-30-linear-foot Madison kitchen somewhere between $5,500 and $12,000. Painted shaker doors and wood-grain laminate surfaces sit at the lower end; real-wood veneer, glass inserts, and specialty finishes sit at the upper end. Refinishing your existing doors instead of replacing them generally runs $2,500 to $6,000. Small projects such as a bathroom vanity or a single wall of cabinets start around $2,500. Every one of those numbers is an estimate to help you budget - we do not quote a kitchen we have not measured, and the written quote we hand you after the in-home measure is the number we stand behind.
