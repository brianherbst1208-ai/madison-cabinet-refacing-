This is the most targeted service we offer. Nothing gets veneered, nothing gets refinished - we replace the doors, the drawer fronts, and where needed the drawer boxes underneath them. It is the right call in three situations: your cabinet frames and finish still look good but a few doors are damaged, your drawers have failed mechanically while the boxes are fine, or you want a style change and your face frames happen to already be painted a color you are keeping.

Drawer boxes are the usual culprit

In Dane County homes built in the 1990s and early 2000s, the drawers fail long before the cabinets do. Stapled particleboard boxes sag and blow out at the bottom, and the old side-mounted rollers only let a drawer open two-thirds of the way, so the back becomes dead storage. We replace those with dovetailed boxes on full-extension soft-close glides. It is the single upgrade customers tell us they notice most in daily use - the drawer opens completely, closes silently, and holds real weight.

Matching versus changing

If you want the new pieces to disappear into an existing finish, be realistic with us: an exact match to a factory finish that has aged ten years under a south-facing window is difficult, and we will tell you honestly how close we expect to get. If matching looks like a losing battle, replacing all the doors and fronts in one consistent new finish usually costs less than you would guess and always looks better than a near-miss.

Why we measure every opening

Cabinet runs are rarely as uniform as they appear, especially in older Madison homes where cabinetry was built on site. We measure each opening individually and note hinge type, overlay, and bore positions. Doors are then built to those measurements. It is the difference between doors that sit flush with even gaps and doors you can tell were ordered off a list.
