Oregon is a village with real range in its housing, and the cabinetry conversation splits cleanly along it. In the older part of town near the village center and along the Jefferson Street area, we work in homes from the early and mid-twentieth century where the kitchen has usually been remodeled at least once. The question there is not style, it is substance: what is actually behind those doors. Some of these kitchens have solid site-built or plywood cabinetry that will reface beautifully. Others have a budget 1980s particleboard remodel with swelling under the sink base and frames that have gone out of square, and refacing those is throwing good money at a box that is failing. We tell you which one you have at the free measure.

On the other side of town, Bergamont and the newer subdivisions off Alpine Parkway are full of homes from the 2000s onward with builder cabinet packages in good condition. Those projects are straightforward style changes: new painted doors and drawer fronts over sound boxes, matching veneer on the face frames, upgraded soft-close hardware, and often a set of pull-outs added while we are in there.

Smaller kitchens, higher stakes per decision

Many Oregon kitchens - particularly in the older village homes and the 1960s and 1970s ranches between the two - are modest in size. That changes our recommendations in a specific way. In a kitchen with sixteen or eighteen linear feet of cabinetry, every foot of storage has to work, so modifications carry more weight relative to refacing than they would in a large open-plan kitchen. Converting fixed shelves to pull-outs, turning a doored base cabinet into a drawer bank, extending uppers into the dead soffit space, and fitting a blind corner with a proper corner solution can make a small Oregon kitchen feel meaningfully larger without moving a single wall. Lighter door finishes help too, and in a smaller room the difference between a warm white and a cool white is very visible.

Straight answers on older homes

Older Oregon homes occasionally hide things behind cabinetry - old plumbing runs, previous water damage, wiring that predates the last remodel. We are a cabinet company, not a general contractor, so when we find something that needs a licensed plumber or electrician, we say so and coordinate rather than working around it and leaving you a problem behind a new door.
